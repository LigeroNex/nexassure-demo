The problem

What AI do we have, and is it still safe to run?

Auditors, regulators and boards ask the same questions: what AI do we have, who approved it, what changed since, can it be broken, and are the controls in place? The answers sit in spreadsheets, test reports and slide packs, and one model update can make all of them out of date.

No reliable inventory

AI systems and bought-in AI services are tracked in spreadsheets, if at all. Nobody can say for sure who approved what.

Agents act, not just answer

They call tools, read records and book things. Ordinary tests miss what an agent can be made to do.

Checks and tests sit apart

A configuration check cannot show whether an agent can be talked into misusing a permission. An attack test cannot show whether logging is on.

Approvals expire silently

The approval was for the version that was checked. A new model, tool or prompt quietly makes it out of date.

How it works

Three parts, one evidence pack.

NexAssure is one product. Each part answers one of the questions, and all three feed the same pack.

  1. 01

    Register

    What AI do we have, who approved it, and what changed?

    Every AI system and agent you run or buy, as you declare it by hand, by CSV or by API: owners, vendors, versions, approvals, classifications, incidents and changes. A model-change watch flags when a model changes under an approval.

  2. 02

    Guard

    Can this agent be made to do something it should not, and can we prove it has stopped?

    Versioned attack scenarios run several times each in an isolated sandbox, with decoy data and mocked tools. Four fixed checks read every step. Findings come with their trace and are retested after the fix.

  3. 03

    Trust

    Are the controls in place?

    A signed runner inside your boundary checks each control against the system as deployed, using your own credentials. Every result is written to a hash-chained record that cannot be quietly changed.

  4. 04

    Evidence pack

    Can we show this to someone entitled to doubt us?

    The register record, Guard's rates and findings, and Trust's control states, side by side, with what was not exercised or not evaluated in plain view. A control that the attack test bears on is evaluated from it, in the same pack.

When something changes, the evidence follows. A change to a model, prompt, tool or permission marks every result that depends on it as stale and schedules a Guard retest and a Trust re-check. It never withdraws an approval by itself: what happens next is decided by named people.

Limits

What NexAssure never does.

An assurance product is only worth having if it is honest about its limits. These are rules, not settings, and we put them in writing at the start of every engagement.

  • No scoreNothing is reduced to one number, grade or percentage. You get rates, control states and counts, with what was not evaluated shown beside what was met.
  • No certificationNexAssure certifies nothing and holds no accreditation. A mapping or a classification supports your judgement; it is not a compliance or legal determination.
  • No attack text shownOnly scenario names, references and outcomes are shown. The scenario text stays with the security team and is never shared between customers.
  • No credentials heldControl checks run in a signed runner inside your boundary, with your own credentials. LigeroNex never holds them.
  • Nothing discovered or scannedThe register holds what you declare, by hand, CSV or API. NexAssure does not scan your networks or go looking for systems.
  • Nothing published without releaseExports are built only from fields a named person has marked publishable. Publishing them is always your act.

Hard limits for every test and check

  • Permission firstNo test or check without written authorisation from the system owner and an agreed scope.
  • Sandbox onlyAttacks run only in an isolated sandbox that is destroyed afterwards.
  • Nothing real happensBookings, messages and payments are simulated with mocked tools, and tests use decoy data, not real customer data.
  • Not production by defaultNo checks against production systems or data without explicit, scoped approval.
  • People decideSeverity, accepting a risk and approval are decisions for named people. Whether to deploy stays with you.
  • Yours aloneFindings and attack detail are never shared between customers.
